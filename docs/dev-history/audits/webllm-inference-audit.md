# Audit WebLLM's cache, flags and preflight claims

**WebLLM is fully automatic about the KV cache — allocation, growth, per-request reuse and teardown are all the library's job, and there is nothing to add — but the audit's conclusion "so nothing to do" is wrong, because the one KV thing we do control is the call we already make: `engine.resetChat()` before every batch is redundant, since the engine resets itself whenever the incoming conversation differs from the one in memory, and it is worse than redundant because it writes to KV state without taking the per-model generation lock.** Of the ten recommendations reviewed against `@mlc-ai/web-llm@0.2.89`, five are verified: **`unlimitedStorage`, `stream_options.include_usage`**, and the three "no change" calls (skip resumable generation, skip the service-worker engine, keep the hand-rolled cache manager). Three are partly true — **the KV summary, the 1024-token window, and the thinking flag** — one is refuted (**the claim that WebLLM drops the cache after each request**), and one is overstated (**the GPU preflight**, which cannot gate on VRAM and is partly already done for us before the weights download). The two findings that change the risk picture the most were missed entirely: **prompt overflow throws `ContextWindowSizeExceededError` rather than truncating**, which makes pre-call input budgeting — not prefill chunking — the thing that protects us, and **the thinking-capable subset of our registry is 14 of 59 entries, not 27**, with the two Ministral-Instruct entries using a completely different reasoning mechanism.

## The KV cache is automatic; `resetChat()` is the only KV bug we own

Answering the direct question first: **no, WebLLM needs no KV-cache work on our side.** The buffer is created exactly once per model load by `create_tir_paged_kv_cache`, with one sequence, page size 16, and `max_total_sequence_length` set from the sliding window or the context window (`node_modules/@mlc-ai/web-llm/lib/index.js:12035-12048`). There is no growth path anywhere in the shipped bundle, no per-request allocation, and the caller has no KV handle to manage — `unload()` disposes the pipeline and the cache with it (`lib/index.js:18470-18488`). Lifecycle is also automatic: `preparePrefillInput` compares the incoming conversation with the in-memory one and resets the pipeline whenever they differ (`lib/index.js:19234-19265`), logging `"Multiround chatting, reuse KVCache."` only in the reuse branch (`lib/index.js:19265`).

Which is precisely why **our explicit `await this.engine.resetChat()` at `src/offscreen/engines/translation/WebLLMEngine.ts:330` cannot be justified as a safety measure**. Our requests send `[system, user]`, while a completed reply leaves the in-memory conversation at `[system, user, assistant]`; the comparison rejects different message counts, so the engine would have reset on its own before every single one of our batches. The call also carries real cost: `MLCEngine.resetChat()` goes straight to `selectedPipeline.resetChat(keepStats)` with **no `lock.acquire()`** (`lib/index.js:19197-19215`), whereas every generation path acquires the per-model lock first — streaming inside the generator body after `create()` returns (`lib/index.js:18689-18690`) and non-streaming before `_generate` (`lib/index.js:18789-18795`). A reset that lands while a stream is unwinding calls `fclearKVCaches` on the sequence that stream is decoding against (`lib/index.js:12123-12150`). Our `TranslationManager` deliberately allows concurrent leases — its lifecycle queue serialises engine replacement, not translations (`src/offscreen/services/TranslationManager.ts:62-69`), with requests entering through `PipelineOrchestrator` (`src/offscreen/services/PipelineOrchestrator.ts:365`) and Studio OCR (`src/offscreen/services/StudioOcr.ts:235`) — so overlap between two batches is one more tab away, not hypothetical.

The mechanism claim behind "nothing to do" is also refuted on its own terms: **nothing in the engine clears the cache at the end of a request.** `_generate` is "reset interrupt flag → prefill → decode loop" with no teardown (`lib/index.js:18495-18511`), and `asyncGenerate` releases its lock without resetting (`lib/index.js:18686-18750`). Upstream documents the persistence explicitly: "Deletion does not clear the active model's in-memory prefix cache. After normal or resumed completion, an ordinary non-resumable follow-up can reuse a matching in-memory conversation prefix" ([resumable_generation.rst](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlc-ai/web-llm/v0.2.89/docs/user/resumable_generation.rst)). So the correct verdict is narrower and more useful than "auto-managed": **automatic for allocation and reuse, and the one thing we could break by hand is the thing we are handling by hand.**

## Thinking off is the right switch aimed at the wrong registry

The API detail is exactly as claimed and worth recording, because there is precisely one shape that works: `extra_body.enable_thinking`, typed at `lib/openai_api_protocols/chat_completion.d.ts:205-213`, read at exactly one site — `lib/index.js:18779` — and honoured by a strict-false test, `if (genConfig?.enable_thinking === false)` (`lib/index.js:12478`). A top-level `enable_thinking` is silently dropped because the unsupported-field denylist is empty, and `chatOpts`/`reload()` cannot carry it because it is not a `ChatConfig` field. `null` and `undefined` both mean thinking on.

What the audit got wrong is scope. Our registry has **14 entries, not 27, that speak the ` thinking` mechanism**: six Qwen3 ids and eight Qwen3.5 ids in `src/shared/models-registry.json` (59 entries total, keys only `id`/`name`/`vramEstimate`/`engine`). The two Ministral-3-3B-Instruct ids are not part of that set — Mistral ships reasoning as a separate checkpoint that uses a `[THINK]…[/THINK]` system-prompt convention, not an inline block ([Mistral 3 announcement](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-3/), [reasoning SYSTEM_PROMPT.txt](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Ministral-3-14B-Reasoning-2512/blob/main/SYSTEM_PROMPT.txt)) — so 16 is an over-count and 27 is an invention. That base rate matters because **7 of the 9 curated recommended translation models are Qwen3/Qwen3.5** (`src/shared/engineOrdering.ts:63-110`), so the flag is aimed at the path most users take.

The runtime side is unchecked in a way that makes a global flag dangerous: the bundle contains exactly **two** `enable_thinking` occurrences, the `genConfig` builder and the `=== false` branch, and there is no model or template gate anywhere — the package says so in its own docstring, "Currently only allowed to be used for Qwen3 and MiniCPM5 models, though not explicitly checked" (`chat_completion.d.ts:206-212`). When the flag is false the runtime does not suppress thinking; it injects the literal `" thinking\n\n</think>\n\n"` into the assistant reply header, and — the part no one expects — **pushes the same token ids into `outputIds`** (`lib/index.js:12481-12483`), so the block lands at the front of the returned `content` as well as in the rendered prompt; upstream issue [#868](https://github.com/mlc-ai/web-llm/issues/868) captured this on `Qwen3.5-4B` with `enable_thinking: false` and a `json_object` schema. Two consequences for us: our parser already strips exactly that prefix (`src/offscreen/engines/translation/BaseLlmTranslationEngine.ts:738`), so the injected block is handled; and combining thinking-on with a JSON grammar is the genuinely dangerous pair, because the grammar constraints only *sampled* tokens, so a model that wants to think emits whitespace until `max_tokens` — issue #868's second run burned 4051 completion tokens and returned `finish_reason: "length"`.

Verdict: the recommendation is directionally right and the design it implies (a `thinking` capability column on registry entries, mirroring the worker's `thinking?: boolean` at `worker/src/config/providers.ts:98`, consumed as `outputBudgetForRoute` in `src/shared/translation/poolContract.ts:59-63`) is the correct one. But it is a **measurement, not a no-brainer**: the benefit is unquantified on our short, schema-constrained batches, and applying it to a non-thinking template injects out-of-distribution prompt text with no error to catch it.

## GPU preflight splits into one free gate, one real gate, and one guess

The three record fields do exist as claimed (`lib/config.d.ts:169-172`), and **7 of our 59 models carry `required_features: ["shader-f16"]`**, but the timing claim — that an f16-less device downloads gigabytes before failing — is refuted for exactly those seven. The check runs at `lib/index.js:18412-18421`, after the ~6 MB wasm fetch and *before* the weight download at `lib/index.js:18438`, so the wasted bytes are single-digit megabytes plus a config JSON. A preflight f16 gate therefore buys a clearer error message and a cleaner "this model is not for this GPU" signal, not bandwidth.

The one requirement that genuinely fails after the weights are on disk is the manifest buffer check: `LLMChatPipeline` validates `compiled.resources.max_storage_buffer_binding_size` in its constructor (`lib/index.js:11863-11881`), which runs at `lib/index.js:18442-18450` — downstream of `fetchTensorCache`. In our registry that path only exists for the two `gemma-4-E2B-it` records (the only ids with a `model_manifest`) at 192 MiB, and its requirement is readable from the adapter for free before download.

VRAM is where the recommendation cannot be implemented as stated. `vram_required_MB` appears 175 times in the bundle and every occurrence is a catalog literal, the last at `lib/index.js:3164`; nothing reads it at load, and no WebGPU API reports device memory — the closest probes are `getMaxStorageBufferBindingSize()` and `getGPUVendor()` (`lib/engine.d.ts:113-114`), and adapter limits are deliberately quantised for fingerprinting resistance ([MDN GPUAdapter.limits](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUAdapter/limits)). Our probe reads neither: `src/offscreen/utils/hardware.ts:46-52` calls `requestAdapter({powerPreference:'high-performance'})`, `requestDevice()` and `destroy()`, then caches success — `features` and `limits` are never touched, and `src/` contains zero references to `required_features`, `vram_required_MB` or `buffer_size_required_bytes`. Adding those two synchronous reads is nearly free; using them to *block* a 6 GB model on the strength of an offline estimate that counts float16 params as 4 bytes would produce false rejections on capable machines. So: hard gate on `adapter.features.has('shader-f16')` and on the 192 MiB buffer requirement, advisory label on VRAM, and let `DeviceLostError` remain the ground truth for OOM.

## Two cheap levers: unlimitedStorage and a 2048-token window

`unlimitedStorage` is missing from `manifest.json:53-60`, which lists `activeTab`, `storage`, `contextMenus`, `offscreen`, `identity` and `declarativeNetRequestWithHostAccess`; the built manifest matches. Per Chrome, the permission "provides an unlimited quota for `chrome.storage.local`, `IndexedDB`, `Cache Storage`, and `Origin Private File System`" ([permissions list](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/permissions-list)) and "exempts extensions from both quota restrictions and eviction" ([Storage and cookies](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/storage-and-cookies)). Without it, WebLLM's default `cacheBackend: "cache"` (`lib/index.js:988-989`) puts multi-gigabyte weights in the extension origin's CacheStorage (`webllm/model`, `webllm/config`, `webllm/wasm`, mirrored in `src/shared/constants.ts:30`) under a 60 %-of-disk quota, where eviction — should it happen — is **whole-origin, not per-file** ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)). The audit's "silent multi-GB re-download" overstates the quota half (a `QuotaExceededError` fails the load visibly and `initProgressCallback` shows the re-download) but understates the eviction half: the same origin holds the Studio project data our app already monitors with `navigator.storage.estimate()` (`src/studio/state/projectStore.ts:254-284`). We in fact already ship the manual version of this mitigation — a user-triggered `navigator.storage.persist()` button (`src/studio/state/projectStore.ts:293-303`, wired at `src/studio/projects/LibrarySidebar.tsx:54-62`) — which is grant-heuristic-dependent and opt-in, whereas the manifest permission is deterministic and covers the model caches the user never thinks about.

The context-window recommendation is right about the arithmetic, right about the mechanism, and wrong about both the number and the scope. It is not a Qwen problem: joining `src/shared/models-registry.json` against `prebuiltAppConfig.model_list` shows **53 of our 59 records pin `context_window_size: 4096` at load** (50 plain, 3 alongside `sliding_window_size: -1`), the 6 `-1k` variants pin 1024, and not one is left un-overridden, so the 4096-versus-1024 mismatch covers 90 % of the catalog. Mechanically the override is a plain key in the record's `overrides` (e.g. `lib/index.js:1917-1931` for Qwen3.5-0.8B, `1816-1831` for Qwen3-0.6B), the merge order `{config, record.overrides, chatOpts}` lets a load-time `chatOpts` win (`lib/index.js:18368`), and our `this.maxContextWindow = 1024` (`WebLLMEngine.ts:183`) is pure JS-side bookkeeping that never reaches the engine — `CreateMLCEngine` is called with no third argument at `WebLLMEngine.ts:272-276`. So the KV cache really is allocated for 4096 slots while our planner refuses to exceed 1024. But setting it to exactly 1024 has a sharp edge the audit does not mention: **the window is a hard ceiling on prompt tokens and overflow throws** `ContextWindowSizeExceededError` (`lib/index.js:14071-14075`, class at `lib/index.js:647-655`), while our input budget is a character-weighted heuristic (`src/shared/translation/tokenEstimation.ts:60-85`) feeding `maxContextWindow - chunkInputTokens - 16` (`BaseLlmTranslationEngine.ts:396-401`). Believing an estimator at 100 % of the window turns an under-count into a lost batch. 2048 keeps the 4×-to-2× saving with real slack. Note also that KV is only one of two state allocations for Qwen3.5: the recurrent half is sized from `max_history_size: 1` in the same catalog override and allocated by `create_rnn_state` (`lib/index.js:12020`, `12049-12056`), so the footprint is not purely window-proportional.

Worth recording for the record: the audit's sibling claim that the `-cs1k` library name encodes an unraisable 1024-token *context* is refuted — `cs1k` is the compiled prefill chunk of 1024 read from wasm metadata (`lib/index.js:11986-11991`, `12044`), which upstream pairs with a 4096 window. And chunked prefill is automatic below the window (`lib/index.js:12525`), while overflow throws before chunking ever matters — so input budgeting, which we already do, is the protection, and prompt splitting for *prefill-chunk* reasons would be wasted effort.

## Usage counts cost one field; the two rejected options stay rejected

`stream_options: { include_usage: true }` is exactly right and our code simply omits it: `makeStreamUsageChunk` returns `undefined` unless `request.stream_options?.include_usage === true` (`lib/index.js:18608-18611`), and once it is set, `usage.completion_tokens` and `prompt_tokens` arrive on a final `choices: []` chunk (`lib/index.js:18613-18640`). "We count deltas" mis-describes our reader — `WebLLMEngine.ts:154` already reads `chunk.usage?.completion_tokens` when present, and our `chunk.choices?.[0]` access tolerates the terminal empty-choices chunk, so the change is safe. Value today is small: the only consumer is the dev-mode tok/s line (`WebLLMEngine.ts:394-400`). Its real value is as the instrument that makes claims 3 and 4 measurable — per-batch exact counts instead of an estimate — which is why it belongs in the first tranche rather than the second.

Resumable generation is a hard reject, and the library says so itself: `ResumableGenerationCoordinator` throws `"Resumable generation does not support grammar-constrained response formats."` for any `json_object`, `grammar` or `structural_tag` request (`lib/index.js:16019-16023`, `17524`), and our request sets exactly `response_format: { type: 'json_object', schema }` (`WebLLMEngine.ts:351-355`). The service-worker engine is a real alternative *host* for WebGPU inference — `CreateServiceWorkerMLCEngine` is a client proxy for a handler in the extension SW, and upstream ships an MV3 example — but it has no DOM, is killable at any point, and could only ever host the LLM: our offscreen document also runs OCR, inpainting and colourisation (`src/offscreen/index.ts:772-774`), and the repo's own invariant keeps WebGPU probes offscreen and high-performance-only (`docs/development/workarounds.md:41-42`). Keeping the hand-rolled `WebLlmCacheManager` is likewise correct, and for the stated reasons: upstream's deleter removes shards listed in `tensor-cache.json` but never the manifest itself (`lib/index.js:7121-7131`), re-fetches that manifest over the network when it is missing (`lib/index.js:6671-6684`), and removes a `model_lib` other records share — all three handled explicitly in `src/offscreen/services/WebLlmCacheManager.ts:52-73`, `88-116`, `164-177`.

## What to adopt, in the order the effort pays back

**No-brainers, all four small and independently reversible.** First, delete `WebLLMEngine.ts:328-330`: the comment "Reset multi-round chat history so each translation transaction is stateless" describes behaviour the engine already guarantees (`lib/index.js:19248-19265`) while the call bypasses the generation lock (`lib/index.js:19197-19215`); expected effect is the removal of a latent KV-corruption path under concurrent batches, at zero behavioural cost — per-request token accounting is also unaffected, because `resetGenerationRoundState()` zeroes `outputIds`, `outputMessage` and the `curRound*` counters at the start of every prefill (`lib/index.js:12279-12291`, `12425`). Second, add `unlimitedStorage` to `manifest.json:53-60`: expected effect is exemption from origin quota and whole-origin eviction for the model caches and Studio data, with no host-permission widening. Third, add `stream_options: { include_usage: true }` to the request object at `WebLLMEngine.ts:341-355`: expected effect is real token counts in the dev log and, more importantly, the instrument needed to settle the next two items. Fourth, pass `chatOpts` `{ context_window_size: 2048 }` as the third argument at `WebLLMEngine.ts:272-276`: expected effect is halving the KV slot reservation (from 4096) while keeping 2× slack over the 1024-token planner, verifiable in one run by watching for `ContextWindowSizeExceededError` at load-adjacent batch boundaries and by comparing `vram_required_MB`-style allocations before and after.

**Measure first, adopt only with numbers.** `enable_thinking: false` needs a `thinking` capability column on the 14 Qwen3/Qwen3.5 registry entries (the Ministral-Instruct ids excluded), then a paired run on one Qwen3.5 model measuring completion tokens, `finish_reason` distribution and parse failures with and without the flag. The GPU preflight needs a decision about *labelling* versus *blocking*: an f16 gate on the 7 records and the 192 MiB buffer gate for `gemma-4-E2B-it` are correct and cheap, a VRAM block is not implementable honestly (`lib/index.js:3164`, `lib/engine.d.ts:113-114`).

**Reject outright.** Resumable generation (hard throw with our schema), the service-worker engine (no DOM, killable, and the pipeline is bigger than the LLM), and replacing the cache manager with `deleteModelAllInfoInCache` (manifest leak, network dependency, shared wasm).

## Conclusion

The productive reframing is that this package's KV cache is not a resource we manage but a resource our *protocol* shapes: because every batch is an independent `[system, user]` conversation, the engine resets between batches whether we ask it to or not, and the only KV decisions left are how big to allocate at load and whether to keep hand-clearing it under a running generation. The audit's instinct — "nothing to do about KV" — lands on the right policy for the wrong reason, and its two best recommendations (`unlimitedStorage`, `include_usage`) are the ones that cost almost nothing, while its two most confident-sounding ones (a VRAM preflight and a 1024-token window) are the ones the platform cannot support as stated. The deeper lesson for the rest of the GPU path is that **`vram_required_MB` is documentation, `required_features` is enforced before download, and `ContextWindowSizeExceededError` is the real wall** — which is why the guard that matters is the input budget we already keep, and why measuring beats guessing wherever the library offers a counter.
